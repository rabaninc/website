# Raban website

Dieses Repo ist die oeffentliche Website von **Raban** (Domain raban.ai).

Der Kontext des Startups — Positionierung, Angebot, ICP, Branding, Sprachregeln —
liegt NICHT hier, sondern im Wissens-Repo `raban-hq/raban` (zugleich Obsidian-Vault).
Wer hier Text schreibt, holt sich die Substanz von dort: `Kontext/Positionierung.md`,
`Kontext/Angebot.md`, `Kontext/Branding.md`. Dessen Regeln gelten auch hier,
besonders **„Belegt oder geraten"** und **„Nichts geht ohne Gruender-Freigabe nach
aussen"**: Diese Seite ist Aussenkommunikation, jeder Satz darauf braucht eine
Freigabe.

**Dieses Repo ist oeffentlich.** Keine Kundendaten, keine Zugangsdaten, keine
internen Ueberlegungen im Code oder in Kommentaren.

## Stand

Der Code stammt aus einem frueheren eigenen Projekt der Gruender (privates
Repo, hier bewusst nicht verlinkt): die
Gestaltungssprache, die Navigationsleiste, die Fusszeile, der Hell-Dunkel-Schalter
und das Seitenraster, dazu der Globus. **Nicht übernommen** wurden
Anmeldung/Konten, die `/backdoor`-Oberfläche und sämtliche personenbezogenen
Daten.

**Der Globus liest den Besucher-Standort.** `utils/visitor-geo.ts` holt das
LAND des Besuchers aus Vercels Edge-Headern — pro Anfrage, nichts gespeichert,
kein Cookie, nie auf Stadt-Ebene. Das macht die Startseite dynamisch (`ƒ` statt
statisch) und **muss in der Datenschutzerklärung stehen**: der Abschnitt
`globe-location` ist dort angelegt und noch leer. Wer den Globus entfernt,
entfernt auch den Abschnitt — und umgekehrt.

**Texte und Grafiken:** Die Startseite liest unter dem Globus das Pitch-Deck
(Claude-Design-Projekt „Raban Pitch v2") als zwei Kapitel: **Problem** (Folie 2,
wo Wissen liegt) und **Lösung** (Folien 5–7: so funktioniert Raban,
Verifikationsschleife, Eigentum und Preis). Die beiden Statistik-Folien (1 von
3, 3 Millionen) sind bewusst draußen — die Komponenten liegen noch unter
`app/components/pitch/` (`one-in-three.tsx`, `next-four-years.tsx`), falls sie
zurückkommen; die Schlussfolie „Pilot im September" ebenfalls draußen
(investorengerichtet, zeitgebunden). `/product` wiederholt 5–7, `/about` zeigt
Folie 8 (Team) in der Anordnung der Folie (eine engere, zentrierte Gruppe wurde am 2026-09-07 ausprobiert und als zu gedrängt verworfen); `/contact` trägt unter der Adresskarte eine zweite Karte, die sagt, dass Raban ein zweites Pilotunternehmen sucht (`app/(public)/contact/pilot-card.tsx`). Eine Folie ist eine Sektion in
`app/components/pitch/sections.tsx`: Titel, Grafik in voller Inhaltsbreite,
darunter zwei bis drei Sätze Kundentext — was ein Käufer aus dieser Folie
wissen muss, verdichtet aus dem Pitch-Skript und an den Leser („Sie")
adressiert; nicht das Skript selbst. Die
SVG-Grafiken sind auf die Farb-Tokens der Seite portiert (Akzent = `red-600`,
hellere Stufen als Deckkraft davon) und zeichnen ihre Linien mit
`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` in festen 2px (eine 1px-Linie verschmiert,
sobald sie nicht auf einem halben Pixel sitzt; 2px decken immer zwei ganze
Pixel). Jede Grafik hat zwei Layouts: die Folie (ab `lg`) und eine schmale
Fassung für Handy und Tablet, die dieselben Teile in gleicher Größe
untereinander stapelt — ein SVG skaliert als ein Bild, die Folie allein würde
auf dem Handy samt Beschriftung auf ein Fünftel schrumpfen. Beide Fassungen
teilen sich die gezeichneten Bauteile; nur die Anordnung (und bei der
Verifikationsschleife: eine Vierteldrehung) unterscheidet sich. Die Bilder der Team-Folie liegen in `app/assets/`. Die Seite ist
zweisprachig: Ohne Cookie entscheidet die Browsersprache (`Accept-Language`:
Deutsch oder Englisch, sonst Deutsch), die `D`/`E`-Kapsel in der Fußzeile
schaltet über ein Cookie um (`utils/locale.ts`, `utils/locale-server.ts`);
Navigation, Fußzeile, Seiten und Grafiken tragen ihre Texte als `de`/`en`-Paar
direkt in der Datei. Die Kapitel der Startseite stehen `--content-gap`
auseinander statt `--header-gap` — Grafiken in voller Breite brauchen mehr
Luft als Fließtext; das ist die eine bewusste Ausnahme von der Abstandsregel
unten. Nach unten schließt die Startseite mit `--inset` statt
`--content-gap` ab — demselben Abstand, den aller Inhalt zu den Seiten hält
(siehe Design language, Page layout), damit die letzte Karte rundum gleich
weit vom Rand sitzt. Alle diese Texte sind Entwurf und brauchen vor dem Livegang noch die
Gründer-Freigabe — wie jeder Satz hier. Das `Placeholder`-Bauteil bleibt für künftige offene
Stellen im Repo; vor jedem Livegang muss `grep -rn "<Placeholder" app` leer
zurückkommen.

**Offene Punkte vor dem Livegang:** Impressum und Datenschutz ausfüllen (§ 5 DDG
verlangt einen echten Anbieter mit Postanschrift), Postfach `kontakt@raban.ai`
einrichten, Abschnitt `globe-location` in der Datenschutzerklärung schreiben.
Das Favicon ist bewusst dasselbe Zeichen wie im Vorprojekt.

# Design language

The look is **organic, flat paper**. Keep it simple — prefer the smallest change that fits.

**One distinction, not two — with one deliberate exception.** Where something has to read as different — the current entry in a list, a label above the links it heads — change *one* property and stop. The section index is toned, not also weighted; the footer's group labels are separated from their links by tone, not also by weight. Stacking signals rarely makes the difference clearer, it just adds a variable every later decision has to track.

The exception is the **document hierarchy**: `<h1>` and `<h2>` carry both size *and* weight (`font-black` and `font-semibold`). A heading is doing two jobs at once — separating itself from body copy, and ranking against the other headings — and size alone had to stretch to cover both. (History: the weights were stripped once, on the one-distinction reading, and put back deliberately: the hierarchy read flatter than it should. Don't strip them again.) Note the exception is about *rank*, not emphasis; `font-semibold` on an inline run inside body copy — a bullet's "Purpose:" lead-in, a table header — is the plain one-distinction case, since type there can't change size.

**One theme: light.** The site ships light only, and every colour is a token in `app/globals.css`. (History: from 2026-09-03 to 2026-09-06 it shipped a dark theme too — every token a `light-dark()` pair picked by `color-scheme`, a capsule switch in the footer pinning `[data-theme]` on `<html>`, a render-blocking inline script in `app/layout.tsx` re-applying the pin before paint, a redefined `dark:` variant, and an invert filter on the team drawings. The founders took all of it out on 2026-09-06 for one look with no second palette to keep in step; the old arms and the switch are in git. Notes below that still say "in both themes" or "the dark arm" describe that removed theme — the light value is the one that stands.)
- **Never write a colour outside `app/globals.css`.** Literal values live in the token block there and nowhere else; `black`, `white`, `#fff`, `neutral-900` and friends are banned everywhere else in `app/**` — the two exceptions are `red-600` (the functional accent) and `neutral-500` (the chrome hover dim). Everything else goes through the Tailwind colours `@theme inline` publishes — **`paper`**, **`ink`**, **`card`**, **`slab`**, **`slab-ink`**, **`on-accent`** — as `text-ink`, `bg-paper`, `border-ink/10`, `bg-slab`. Opacity modifiers work as normal. `inline` is what makes a token edit the whole change: it emits `var(--ink)` into each utility rather than baking in today's value. Don't drop it.
- **`color-scheme: light`** stays on `:root` so the browser draws its own surfaces — scrollbars, form controls, the native focus ring — light whatever the device prefers; without it a dark-mode Mac paints a dark scrollbar on the white page.
- **The footer slab is the accent red.** `--slab: #dc2626` (Tailwind's `red-600`, the red the pitch graphics fill their blocks with) and `--slab-ink: var(--on-accent)` (white). A mid-luminance red ends the page with a block that is emphatically *not* the paper, and ties the site to the deck, which ends its slides on this red.
- **No `dark:` variant.** Tailwind's stock one still exists and must not be used: a `dark:` utility anywhere would re-introduce a second look by the back door.
- **No `theme-color` meta.** Safari tints its chrome from the page's own background when none is set, which is right as it is.


**Surface & color**
- **One colour.** The palette is achromatic — black, white, and neutral greys — plus a single hue: the pitch deck's **red** (`red-600`, `#dc2626`). It appears in exactly three places: the filled blocks of the pitch graphics, the footer slab (`--slab`), and the destructive actions and error text in `/backdoor`. Never introduce a second hue, and never put this one on anything else, and never warm a grey (no stone/amber/taupe — use `neutral-*` or plain hex greys). Achromatic paper and ink are what make the two themes one design: with no hue to shift, dark is a clean inversion rather than a re-colouring — and the one red stays put in both, so it never needs a dark arm.
- Paper background `--paper` (set in `app/globals.css`), **full-strength ink text** — resting *static* text is always `text-ink`, never a muted grey. Build de-emphasis from size / weight / spacing, not tone. The one exception is **a control that has no other affordance**, which rests at half ink and resolves to full under the pointer (see **Interaction** below) — the footer's links and the section index, and that is the whole list. It is *not* a blanket rule for anything clickable: a control that already marks itself some other way — a highlight box, the navbar's dim — stays at **full ink**, because half-toning it as well says the same thing twice and dims a live button for no gain. The **section index** on `/privacy` and `/legal` is the same rule read as a list — thirteen equally black entries have no focus, so the entries rest at `ink/50` and the one you're reading is the resolved one. Tone is never the way to grade a block of *static* text against itself. (History: the paper was once a warm off-white `#faf4ef`; it was flattened to pure white in the achromatic redesign — don't reintroduce the tint. Dark mode came later and changed none of this: it re-pointed the same rules at the same tokens.)
- **Flat** — content carries **no drop shadows**. Structure comes from thin **ink hairline borders/rules**, not elevation. (Cards are the softer exception — see **Cards**.)
- **The navbar is the one exception: it is lifted, not flat.** (The page card below hangs the same way and casts onto the footer — see **The page hangs from the top**.) It reads as a thin pane of sand-brushed glass overlaying the page from the top, and it gets that weight two ways:
  - **A polished bottom edge, and NO hairline.** The bar has no `border-b` at all: `--edge-polish` (`inset 0 -3px 5px -3px rgba(0,0,0,0.09)`) is the entire edge, grading the ink upward over ~3px so the boundary is a graded lip rather than a drawn line. **A border and this gradient don't coexist well** — a hard 1px terminator under a soft gradient reads as two edges, and glass polished round has no hard terminator anyway. The silhouette survives without it: the polish darkens the bottom row inside the bar, and `--lift` shades the paper just outside it. **This must be an inset shadow, never a gradient border:** `border-image` with a `linear-gradient` cannot follow `border-radius` — it would square off the rounded corners — while an inset shadow hugs the curve. (History: the edge went 2px → 1.5px → 1px → none, getting lighter at every step. Note also that **border widths quantize to whole device pixels**, so on 1× and 2× screens nothing sits between 1px and 1.5px — a `[1.25px]` border just computes to 1px.) Keep the polish faint — at `0.30` it stopped reading as a polished lip and became a second, blurry line, and `0.15` was still heavier than glass wants. The page card takes no polish — its edge is its cast, see **The page hangs from the top**. The footer takes none: its top is the slab, not paper, and there is nothing there to polish.
  - **A long, wide, very faint drop shadow, lit from directly overhead**: `--lift: 0 8px 20px` at `0.10` black, in `app/globals.css`. It reaches roughly 18px down the page. A shadow that hugs the bar reads as a hard opaque object lying flat on the paper; length is what sells the pane as *held above* the page. The ink stays very low even as the throw grows — glass scatters what little it blocks, so long and faint, never long and dark. Strictly, an overhead light on a card parallel to the page hides its own shadow and leaks only an even penumbra; the small offset is the deliberate stylistic part — it's what makes the bar read as lifted. **This is the app's only drop shadow** — do not spread the effect to cards, content or the page card's edge. (History: it started at `0 6px 16px` / `0.16` — the right *length*, far too much ink — then overcorrected to `0 2px 5px` / `0.07`, light but so concentrated it read as a hard edge. The geometry that works is the long one at the low alpha. The light arm then sat at `0.03` for a while, and on a phone the dark bar visibly threw more shadow than the light one; `0.10` is the midpoint of two perceptual models' answers — see the token's comment for the model behind it — and is meant to be judged on a phone.)
  - The phone navcard takes the same handover as the frost: when it's open the bar drops its shadow (`max-sm:shadow-none`) and the card carries the lift and the polish, so the two never stack into a doubled edge.
- **The page hangs from the top, and the slab is behind it.** The page itself is a card: paper, square where it meets the navbar, rounded where it ends — the navbar's own reading one level down. So `--radius` sits on the content div in `app/(public)/layout.tsx` and `bg-slab` on the layout wrapper, which is what shows through the two corner notches the curve cuts out of the paper. **The card's edge is its cast, not a line:** `--page-cast` alone makes the edge (see below); no hairline runs along it. (History: a 0.5px `--edge-line` border sat on the card's sides and bottom for a while — the half-point frame line a macOS window draws around itself — and was tuned through alphas from 10% to 90% in both themes. It never landed: paler it read as nothing against the red, darker as an outline, and in Safari the card's fractional bottom edge smeared it over two device rows regardless. Removed; don't bring it back.) The footer is square because it is no longer a card: it's the surface the page hangs over, continuing below where the card stops. (History: the footer used to be the card, rising from the bottom on rounded top corners. Don't put them back — the page reads as one object now, not two meeting in the middle.)
  - **The card carries `overflow-clip`.** A `border-radius` shapes an element's own background but does **not** clip its descendants, and a descendant on its own composited layer will happily paint outside that curve — the globe animates on a `transform`, so while it runs it is composited, and its box ends exactly on the card's bottom edge at the end scale. **Clip, not hidden** — `hidden` would make the card a scroll container and re-anchor the sticky section index to it (same reasoning as the `overflow-x: clip` on html/body). It costs nothing otherwise: the globe exactly fills its section at every breakpoint and is smaller than it while animating, so the clip only ever bites at the two notches. (It is *not* what fixed the corner flash — see the next bullet. A live composited descendant was verified to be clipped correctly by it, which is what ruled that cause out.)
  - **The card's edge is the cast, and no lip.** `--page-cast` is the edge — the shadow a macOS window throws onto what is behind it. (History: the edge was bare for a while, the curve alone; an inset `--page-lip` sat on the card's bottom row — first a straight alias of `--edge-polish`, then cut deeper to `inset 0 -6px 10px -5px` and inked up to 40% black in light / 60% white in dark. It was removed: at the navbar's numbers it vanished against the slab, and at numbers you could see it read as a drawn band along the curve rather than a sanded edge. The navbar's `--edge-polish` is now the only lip in the app.)
    **The card casts onto the footer:** `--page-cast` (`0 12px 32px` black at 0.28 plus a tight `0 1px 3px` seat at 0.18, the two layers the OS draws) sits on the card in `app/(public)/layout.tsx` with `relative z-10` so it paints over the footer that follows it in the DOM. One arm, black in both themes: the red slab it lands on does not flip. This is the whole edge, in both themes. (History: an earlier `--page-cast` — `--lift`'s `0 8px 20px` geometry, a white glow at 0.55 in light and black at 0.55 in dark — was thrown from a static, empty `bottom-full` element after `<Footer />` onto the black/white slab of the time, and was removed because the smear read as a stain on the slab. The boundary element existed because the globe's composited layer had flashed a square silhouette through the corner notches when a cast sat on the card itself; if that flash returns, that is the first place to look. The red slab is what makes a cast read as depth now: dark red under a shadow is still red.)
- **The footer takes no shadow of its own.** Its only box-shadow is the structural 1px one that covers the sub-pixel canvas sliver — it paints `--slab`, not black, because in dark the slab is lighter than the paper and a black line there would draw an edge instead of hiding one. It needs no lift of its own; the card above casts onto it (`--page-cast`, see the bullet above). (History: a mirrored `--lift-up` was tried above the footer back when the footer was the card, and dropped — an upward shadow onto paper with nothing to catch it reads as a smudge rather than an edge.)
- **Rounded corners** throughout (soft, not sharp). One radius var drives the chrome: `--radius: 28px`, applied as `rounded-b-[var(--radius)]` on the navbar, the navcard and the page card; the footer itself is square. **The radius is exactly half the bar height** — the curve resolves at the bar's midline, so the ends read as one clean arc; past half (a 32px curve on a 48px bar) too little straight edge is left and the ends bulge. Change `--nav-h` and the radius must follow it. `--gutter` is `var(--radius)`, so a label starts exactly where the corner curve straightens out — at a smaller inset the first character reads as sitting inside the bend. Cards keep their own, smaller `rounded-2xl` (16px), a clear step below the chrome. **The chrome corners are plain circular arcs**, the same corner a macOS window draws — no `corner-shape`. (History: the navbar, the navcard and the page card carried `[corner-shape:superellipse(1.5)]` for a while — a curve that starts its bend at zero curvature, which hides the one point where a circle goes from straight to curved. It was dropped to match the OS window the site sits in: side by side, the superellipse read as smoother than the window and therefore as *not the same kind of corner*. Don't bring it back on one element only — a page corner and a navbar corner in two different shapes reads as two chromes.)
- **Frosted-glass navbar** is the other deliberate non-flat quality (with the lift above): a translucent **paper veil** — a top-to-bottom gradient of the paper colour from `paper`/40% to /15% + a 24px backdrop blur (the `frost` utility in `app/globals.css`, NOT Tailwind's `backdrop-blur-xl`: macOS Safari drops Tailwind's var()-composed backdrop-filter and shows tint with no blur — a WebKit bug, tailwindlabs/tailwindcss#18765 — so the blur is written out as a literal; `frost-none` is its off switch), so content fades as it scrolls under. **Real glass scatters light; it doesn't paint over it.** When the frost needs to be subtler, take it out of the *paper fill* and put it into the *blur* — a heavy fill is opaque plastic, a heavy blur with a thin fill is glass. The fill exists only to keep the bar's own text legible over whatever passes beneath. The veil tints with the exact background colour (`--paper`), so the frost reads as the same paper, just blurred. Keep it. (History: a `backdrop-saturate-150` once warmed the blur; it was dropped because it shifted the frost warmer than the flat paper beside it — the goal is the frost flush with the background, not a cast of any kind. Saturating a frost over an achromatic page has nothing to saturate, so it can only introduce a tint.)
  - **Where an effect is physical, let it behave physically** — favour real realism over a faked/flattened approximation (e.g. the navbar frost is a real backdrop-blur that fades page content as it scrolls under, not a painted gradient). Applies app-wide. (Not a licence for shadows/skeuomorphism — the surface still stays flat.) **Exception — the phone navcard:** when open, the navbar drops its own frost so the navcard sheet behind it is one continuous veil; an earlier double-frost (navbar over sheet) was deliberately removed because, full-width, the doubled strip read as a light seam above the divider rather than a charming overlap.
- The footer is a solid block that is not the paper (`bg-slab`, `text-slab-ink` — the accent red with white ink, the same in both themes; see **Two themes**) — **not a bar**, and no longer a card either: it is the slab the page card hangs over, showing below it. It's free to grow downward: nothing measures its height (globe pages size their own sections in `vw`), so it's padded with `--gutter` all round and sized by its contents. It holds **labelled link groups**, declared as data in `GROUPS` at the top of `app/components/footer.tsx` — a new group is one entry there, never new JSX. The groups sit opposite the wordmark at **every** width, phones included — one column fits beside it at 375px, and stacking it underneath wasted the whole right half of the bar; a second group stacks under the first on phones and moves alongside it once there's room. The horizontal gap between columns is deliberately wider than any vertical gap inside them, so they read as separate lists rather than one grid. **The nav routes are not repeated here** — Product/About/Contact live in the navbar on every page, and a footer column duplicating them earned nothing (it was tried and removed). The signed-in-only `/backdoor` link sits under the wordmark rather than in a group: `BackdoorLink` renders null when signed out, and a group of its own would leave a heading standing over nothing.
- **Every dimension is a multiple of 8px** — one grid for the chrome, the gutter and the vertical rhythm, so nothing carries an arbitrary number. Nav height `--nav-h: 56px` (7 units) — still a slim bar, a frame around the page rather than furniture; radius `--radius: 28px` (half the bar, and the **one** dimension off the 8px grid — the half-height rule wins over the grid, since the arc is what the eye reads); page gutter `--gutter` = the radius (28px) so no text touches the screen edge and the navbar label aligns with the page text below it; `--content-gap: 40px` (5u) → `--content-top: 96px` (12u); `--header-gap` = the gutter (28px); `--tagline-top: nav-h + 104px` (13u). (History: the gutter and radius were once 32px against a 48px bar — the fat curve on a slim bar read bulbous; raising the bar to 56px *to match that 32px curve* made the chrome heavy. Halving the radius instead fixed the proportion. The bar is 56px again now, but with the half-height radius that follows from it — 28px, not the 32px that made it read heavy before. The chrome text was also briefly 18px/16px against the 56px bar; it stays at the body 16px.)
- **Page layout (all pages except home):** the page heading sits `--content-top` below the top (clearing the navbar), and the first line of content sits `--header-gap` (24px) below the heading. Both are CSS vars in `app/globals.css` — reuse them (`pt-[var(--content-top)]` on `<main>`, `mt-[var(--header-gap)]` on the content block, à la `/privacy` and `/legal`) rather than hardcoding, so the rhythm stays shared. **Sides: content keeps `--inset` to the screen edge, the chrome keeps `--gutter`.** Every `<main>` (and the home page's card block) is `px-[var(--inset)]` — a fixed **64px** (8 units) from 640px up, `--gutter` below it — while the navbar, the footer and the home hero stay on `--gutter` (28px) and frame the screen. (2026-09-06: first 6.4% of the viewport capped at 128px, matched to a wide editorial reference the founders brought, then three quarters of that, then the fixed 64 the same evening; before that everything sat on the one 28px gutter.) **Content closes with `--inset` at the bottom,** the same distance it keeps to the sides: every `<main>` (and the home page's card block) ends `pb-[var(--inset)]`, so the last thing on a page — a card, a document, the contact card — sits in one even frame of paper before the page card's edge. (History: pages closed with `pb-[var(--content-gap)]` to mirror the gap under the navbar; then the card pages alone closed with `--gutter`, then `--inset`; since 2026-09-06 every page does.) **Vertical spacing between sections must never exceed `--header-gap`** — reuse it (`space-y-[var(--header-gap)]` on the content block) so the gap between top-level sections equals the header→content gap; spacing *within* a section (heading→body, list rows) stays tighter. The home page is the deliberate exception (its hero uses `--tagline-top`).
- **Long documents (`/privacy`, `/legal`) carry a sticky section index** — `SectionIndex` in `app/components/section-index.tsx`, a column out in the page's left margin. **The layout is a three-track grid** — `1fr / minmax(0, var(--measure)) / 1fr` — with the document in the middle track, so the prose is centred **in the viewport** and the index sits beside it in the margin. Don't rebuild this as a centred flex row of index+document: that centres the *pair* and leaves the prose sitting left of centre, which is the bug this replaced. `--measure` (672px, ~70 characters of mono) is the reading measure.
  - **The heading takes a grid row of its own, and that's load-bearing.** With the heading in row 1 and the index and prose both in row 2, `items-baseline` on the grid puts the index's first entry and the first line of body text on **one shared baseline** — the browser measures both line boxes, so 12px text and 16px text align exactly, and stay aligned if either size changes. Put the heading back inside the prose column and the index baselines against a 48px heading instead. Don't reach for a margin offset here: an earlier version used `mt-[var(--index-top)]` to clear the heading, which aligned the two *boxes* but left the two *baselines* 5px apart and assumed the heading never wrapped. Also don't add `self-start` to the index — it overrides the baseline alignment. (Baseline alignment sizes the item to its content, so `sticky` still has room to slide within the row, and the index still comes to rest at `--content-top` under the navbar.)
  - **Below `xl` the index moves into the navbar** as a second row: the bar unrolled by exactly one row, showing which section you're currently in, under the same inset divider the navcard uses, with a chevron (not the navbar's `+`) that expands the full list in place. The top row is untouched.
  - **There is ONE phone/tablet sheet, not one per feature.** The section bar and the navcard's links both unroll from the same `top:0` surface, so they *stack* inside it and the sheet simply grows further down. **The order is fixed:** the site menu sits directly under the top row — it belongs to the bar itself — and the page's section chrome moves down to make room for it, with anything expanded growing downward from there. (History: they were two absolute sheets and opening the `+` had to hide the section bar, because only one of them could own `top:0`. Keep them merged: the section row is the page's own chrome and shouldn't vanish because someone opened the site menu.) Both expansions can be open at once; the tap-outside backdrop closes both, and it only exists while something is expanded, since the section row itself is permanent chrome rather than a modal. It's the exact complement of the column — bar below `xl`, column at `xl` and up — so every viewport has one section index and never two; stopping it at `sm` would leave tablets and small laptops with no way through the document. The navbar hands its surface down to whichever sheet is open (navcard or section bar) exactly as it already did for the navcard, so the two never stack into a doubled frost with a seam at the divider.
  - The bar is **absolute, like the navcard, and contributes no flow height**. The two pages that publish an index reserve the row in their own top padding (`max-xl:pt-…`, and a matching `scroll-mt`) — that padding is server-rendered, so the page opens at its final position instead of jolting down when the bar mounts.
  - `SectionIndex` **publishes** its sections and current id over `"raban-sections"`, the same window-event channel the breadcrumbs use, and the bar pins back over `"raban-section-pin"`. One spy, one active id: the bar has no copy of the tracking logic to drift from, and a tap in the bar gets the same pin treatment a click in the column does — without it, tapping section 12 or 13 lands on 11.
  - **The index appears at exactly `xl`, and the number isn't arbitrary:** the outer tracks are `(100vw - 2*gutter - measure) / 2` wide, which is the room the index has to live in — around 280px at 1280 and 360px at 1440, against a list that measures ~238px. Below `xl` that margin is too narrow to hold the list unwrapped, so the page runs the prose on its own. Note the grid takes **no column gap**: the outer tracks already separate index from prose, and a `gap-x` keeps charging for two gutters after those tracks collapse to zero — which on a phone shrinks the prose by two gutters (56px) and pushes it off the page gutter. The page declares its sections **as data** (`id`, `title`, `body`) and generates both the index and the `<h2>`s from that one array, so the list can't drift from the headings it points at; the **numbers come from array position**, never typed in, so inserting a section renumbers the document and its index together. (The one thing the array can't renumber: cross-references written into the prose — "see section 10".) Each `<section>` carries `scroll-mt-[var(--content-top)]` so an anchor jump clears the navbar, and the spy reads that same computed value back off the element, so the jump and the highlight can't disagree.
  - A thirteen-item list stacked above the content on a phone would push the document itself off the first screen, so below the breakpoint the index is simply gone. On a phone the page is scrolled, not navigated.
  - Entries sit at **12px**, the app's small size — the same step down the footer takes, and for the same reason: an index is read when someone goes looking for it. There's 16px of air between them, matching the footer's `gap-4` at that size.
  - **Every entry has the same hover target, and it's the width of the longest one.** The column is `w-max` (capped `max-w-full`) so it shrinks to its longest entry, and each `<a>` is `block`, so it fills that width however short its own label is. Left inline, a short entry like "5. Server logs" stops responding a third of the way across the column while a long one runs its full width, and the list hands over at a ragged edge that follows the text rather than the column.
  - **That 16px of air is `py-2` on the entries, not `space-y-4` on the list** — so the targets *tile*: every pixel of the column belongs to exactly one entry and nothing dies in the gaps as the cursor travels down. Rendered spacing is identical either way (19.2px line box + 16px = a 35.2px pitch); only the hit area differs. The padding doesn't disturb the baseline alignment above, because `items-baseline` measures the first line box wherever the padding puts it.
  - **Tone carries the state, and hover resolves rather than dims:** entries rest at `text-ink/50` and go to full `text-ink` when hovered *or* current, so the reader always has one live marker of where they are. (History: this was first built full-ink with the current entry in **bold**, to keep resting text at full ink per the rule above — it was overruled deliberately. Weight is too quiet a signal at 12px across thirteen items, and the whole point of an index is that one entry stands out.)
  - The index deliberately **does not go through `LinkStyle`**. Its highlight box is the affordance for proper buttons; thirteen boxes lighting up under the cursor is chrome the page doesn't need. `LinkStyle`'s `chrome` mode doesn't fit either — that's the navbar, which *dims* on hover, and here hover resolves. The index is the original home of the half-ink resolve, and still its clearest case: no box, so the ink is the mark.
  - **A clicked entry pins the highlight** until the reader scrolls for themselves (released on `wheel`/`touchmove`/`keydown`, never on `scroll` — the jump fires that itself). The tail of a document can sit below the line and never reach it: on `/privacy` the last three sections activate past the maximum scroll offset, so position alone can't tell an anchor jump to section 12 from one to section 13. Honouring the click is what makes every entry land on itself; once scrolling has run out, the highlight hands off to the nearest section still below the line rather than parking several screens above.

**Type**
- One face: **Archivo**, the typeface the pitch deck is set in (its Modernist design system runs Archivo 800 for headings, 400 for body), self-hosted through `next/font/google` in `app/layout.tsx` (variable font, latin + latin-ext, published as `--font-sans`) and set on `body` in `app/globals.css` with the system Helvetica stack (`"Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`) behind it as fallback. Body rests at 400; `<h1>` keeps `font-black`, `<h2>` `font-semibold`. Chosen 2026-09-03 by Johannes so site and deck read as one. (History: the site ran on the bare system mono stack, SSI-style, with no web font; then briefly IBM Plex Mono and Inter on the same day. Superseded.)
- Inter is a variable font, so every weight from `font-semibold` to `font-black` is real (no faux-bold). Body sits at **regular (400)** by default.
- **Spacing:** `letter-spacing: 0` (mono's natural width — no added tracking) and `line-height: 1.6`, both on `body`. Tighten leading locally for large display text (the home lede uses `leading-[1.15]`), but keep tracking at 0 — negative tracking fights a monospaced face.
- **The scale is five steps, and it lives in `app/globals.css` — never hardcode a heading size.** Body and navbar at **16px**; a **12px** small size (`text-[12px]`) for the section index, and *everything* in the footer; the home hero at **28px** (`text-[1.75rem]`, its own thing — see below); and the two heading vars, **`--h1`** and **`--h2`**, which every `<h1>`/`<h2>` in the app reads as `text-[length:var(--h1)] leading-[var(--h1-line)]`. Changing the heading scale is therefore one edit in one file, not nineteen.
  - Headings carry **both** the size step and a weight step — `font-black` on `<h1>`, `font-semibold` on `<h2>` — the deliberate exception to the one-distinction rule above. See there for why, and for why it shouldn't be undone again.
  - `--h1` is **48px** on a 56px line box, `--h2` **24px** on 32px; the window cards' slide titles take a sixth step of their own, `--slide`, **32px** on 40px (see **Window cards**). Headings are display type and take a **tightened line box** — mono at 48px on the body's 1.6 leading reads as two loose lines rather than one heading — and both numbers land on the 8px grid, so a heading occupies whole grid units like everything else.
  - **The scale has one breakpoint** (`min-width: 640px`), the only one in the type system: below it `--h1` drops to 32px/40px and `--h2` to 20px. A monospaced face is far wider per character than the proportional faces this scale was drawn against, so a title that sits on one line at desktop breaks into three on a 375px screen. `--h2-line` deliberately **does not** change across the step — only the glyph size does, so a section heading keeps the same 32px line box at either size and a document's vertical rhythm doesn't shift under the breakpoint.
  - The navbar keeps 16px: it's read while navigating. The footer is read only when someone goes looking for it, so it steps down **as a block** rather than shrinking one part of itself; the section index does the same, at the same size.
  - The **home hero stays at 28px** and is not on the heading scale — it's a lede, body copy used as a hero, not a page title. It's the one page with no `<h1>` at all, so nothing there competes with it.
- The footer's **group labels** are full `slab-ink` at the **same size and weight** as the links they head — tone is still the only thing separating them, but the split runs the other way now: the label is static text so it's full, the links are controls so they rest at half. Nothing has to be *held back*; the links recede on their own. Don't bold the label (tried, at `font-bold` — it shouts) and don't dim it: at `slab-ink/50` it would be identical to its own list at rest. (History: it sat at `slab-ink/50` as the single deliberate break in the full-ink rule, from when the links beside it were full ink too. The break is gone, not forgotten.)
- (History: Share Tech Mono was tried via `next/font`, then scrapped for this zero-download system stack. Chrome text was briefly pushed to 18px/16px to match a 56px bar, then returned to 16px with the slim bar.)

**Interaction**
- Links/buttons go through the `LinkStyle` component, and its whole job is **one affordance, never two**. Every control marks itself as a control exactly once, and `LinkStyle` picks which mark:
  - **a highlight box** (the default) — content links and buttons, at **full ink**. The box is already doing the saying.
  - **half ink resolving to full** (`highlight={false}`) — `ink/50 → ink` on paper, `slab-ink/50 → slab-ink` on the slab. The footer's links, which have no box, so the ink itself is the mark. It snaps, like every other hover here.
  - **a dim to `neutral-500`** (`chrome`) — the navbar, a frame present on every route: a permanently half-lit bar reads as a disabled one, so it keeps full ink and marks itself on hover instead. Implies no box.
  - **nothing at all** (`icon highlight={false}`) — the language flip alone, whose letters are their own answer (the theme flip, gone with the dark theme, was the other).
  - So `highlight={false}` means "no box, mark yourself with the ink instead", never "no affordance". And **a glyph never takes the half ink** even with the box off: a word at half strength still reads as the word, while a 16px hairline reads as disabled, with no size or weight left in it to carry what the ink took away. (History: half ink shipped as a blanket rule for everything clickable and was walked back to this in three passes — glyphs, then the flip, then boxed buttons. Don't re-generalise it.)
- **Links out to another site don't go through `LinkStyle`** — there's no room for a highlight box mid-sentence — and are **full ink with an `underline underline-offset-2`**, always. The underline is their one mark, so the ink has none left to do; it's also what a link off the site has looked like since the first web page. **Hover lifts the underline** instead of adding anything: everywhere else in the app hover puts a mark *on*, but here the mark is already there at rest, so taking it away is the one move left that reads — and the word doesn't shift by a pixel doing it. The underline takes **no `decoration-*` colour of its own**, so it's `currentColor` and can't drift from the text. (History: these briefly took the half ink and kept the underline through hover, when half ink was still a blanket rule for anything clickable.)
- The hover affordance is a **soft rounded highlight box** that snaps in/out instantly behind the element (à la the Claude desktop app's icon buttons): faint `ink/6%` on paper, `slab-ink/15%` on the footer. `LinkStyle` applies its styling directly onto the interactive element (via `cloneElement`) so the box and the click target are the same region; its padding is cancelled by equal negative margins to avoid layout shift.
- **Below `lg` — phone and tablet, where the pointer is a fingertip — every `LinkStyle` link grows its hit area by 8px a side**, cancelled by equal negative margins so nothing moves. 8px is chosen rather than rounded up: the footer stacks its links on a `gap-4`, so 8px above and below makes adjacent targets meet *exactly* — neither overlapping (one link stealing the other's edge) nor leaving a dead strip between them. Watch for containers sized to the text: the breadcrumb trail is `overflow-x-hidden`, which forces `overflow-y` to compute as `auto`, so the taller links overflowed it and Chrome painted a scrollbar in the navbar — the trail is `h-[var(--nav-h)]` now so there's nothing to scroll.
- **A drag moves whatever is on top where the finger lands.** The two navbar surfaces — the bar itself and the phone sheet — carry `touch-pinch-zoom`, so a one-finger drag that *starts* on either moves nothing: a thumb resting on the bar, or on an open navcard, used to drag the page underneath it, so reaching for the `+` sent the page somewhere nobody asked it to go. **The tap-outside backdrop deliberately does not.** It's an invisible full-viewport layer, so wherever it is the topmost thing there is nothing covering the page — the page is what you can see and the page is what a drag should move. Below where the sheet ends, scrolling works exactly as it always did, navcard open or not; a tap there still closes the menu, because a tap isn't a pan. Put the property on the surfaces, never on the sticky wrapper: the wrapper contains the backdrop, so locking it locks the whole screen. (History: it was on the wrapper for exactly one commit, and froze the entire viewport whenever the navcard was open.) `pinch-zoom` rather than `none` — two-finger zoom, and panning while zoomed, still work; it's the accidental one-finger pan being taken away, not accessibility. And this governs only where a gesture *begins*: a scroll started on the page is untouched, rubber-band bounce and all, and the bar still rides it.
- **In a stacked list, links are full-width rows** — `LinkStyle`'s `block`, used by both lists in the phone sheet. A link is otherwise only as wide as its label, so "About" or "7. Cookies" stops responding a third of the way across while a long one runs the full width, and the list hands over at a ragged edge that follows the text. The container's `px-[var(--gutter)]` stays *outside* the target: the page's margins are breathing room, not something you should be able to hit. Row spacing goes on the rows as `py`, never as a `gap` on the list, so the targets tile with no dead strip between them — the rendered spacing is identical either way, only the hit area differs. `block` also drops the horizontal touch padding, since a full-width row doesn't need it and `w-full` plus a negative inline margin would push the row out past its own gutter.
- The box is **on by default** for content links/buttons, and sits *on top of* the resolve rather than replacing it — except on `icon` buttons, which have no resolve to sit on and are box alone. The footer's links and its `/backdoor` link pass `highlight={false}`: no box, resolve alone. The **navbar** passes `chrome`: no box and no resolve, **dimming to mid grey** (`neutral-500`, halfway between paper and ink — and the same grey on all four surfaces, which is why it needs no dark arm) on hover/tap instead. The **theme flip** passes `icon highlight={false}` and so gets none of the three — see **The theme flip** above for why. It draws its track and knob in `currentColor` so the capsule is one object whichever colour `LinkStyle` hands it, restated nowhere.

The charts' wide/narrow switch is the viewport's `lg`. (History, 2026-09-06: from `lg` the body was a two-track grid — graphic on the flexible track, copy on a fixed 20–24rem column, sides alternating card by card via an `index` prop, columns first centred then top-aligned — which left every graphic at two thirds of the card; the copy went above the graphic on phones first, then everywhere, for better-proportioned cards.)

- **Surface:** paper (no fill), `rounded-2xl` corners, a **faint** `border border-ink/10` hairline — softer than the structural ink rules so the card frames without shouting. Still flat (no shadow).
